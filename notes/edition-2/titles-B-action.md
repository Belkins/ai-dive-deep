# Title system B — Action Verb / Operator Move

## Philosophy

Every chapter title is a move the reader will execute, not a topic they will study. A scanner sees the verb and the object and knows exactly what behavior changes after the chapter — "Kill Your Tabs," "Schedule One Task Tonight," "Cap Your Bill." Imperative mood, 3-6 words, a receipt number when it sharpens the promise.

## Three you're least sure about

- **Ch 03 — "Treat Every Session as Day One":** This is a mental-model chapter, not a do-this-now chapter. The verb "Treat" is weaker than "Kill" or "Schedule." Alternative: "Onboard Every Session Fresh" — but that's still a frame, not a click. Mental-model chapters resist action-verb framing because the move is internal.
- **Ch 21 — "Know Which Mode You're In":** "Know" is almost a non-verb. The real move is "pick" or "switch," but the chapter is about diagnosis before action. Considered "Pick Your Mode Before You Type" — more specific but loses the diagnostic point.
- **Ch 32 — "Pick an Archetype, Skip the Build":** Slightly clever, slightly compound. The promise (don't build the agent, pick a preset) is real, but the title may read as two ideas. Cleaner alt: "Use a Preset Agent." Lost some punch in the trim.
