# Title system C — Question / Reader Intent

## Philosophy

Every chapter title is the exact phrase a reader types into Cmd-K at 11:47pm when they hit the wall. The chapter is the answer; the title IS the question — in the reader's voice, not Vlad's. Most are intent-shaped ("how do I", "when should I", "what if") because that's how operators actually search; a few are statements that imply the question because forcing "how" on every line makes the table read like a survey.

## Three you're least sure about

- **Ch 10 — "What would I actually build with agents tomorrow morning?"** This chapter is a grab-bag (hosted vs self-hosted, Rick, Ollama, the travel prompt). No single reader question covers all four. The title lands on "tomorrow morning" intent but sacrifices coverage of local-models and Rick — those readers won't find this chapter via Cmd-K. A subtitle has to do the work.
- **Ch 17 — "What do I only learn at hour 200 that I could've known at hour 2?"** Tips-and-tricks chapters resist intent shaping because the reader doesn't have a specific question — they have a vague "what am I missing." The hour-200/hour-2 framing is the closest natural question, but it's longer than ideal (12 words) and reads more like a Vlad-voice koan than a reader DM.
- **Ch 31 — "In what order do I actually build a thing?"** "The Stages" is a framework chapter, and frameworks always read better as statements than questions. The question version works but feels slightly thin — a reader looking for "shipping framework" or "six stages" won't recognize this in the grid. Candidate alternative: "I have an idea on Friday — what do I do first?"
