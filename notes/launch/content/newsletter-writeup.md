# Newsletter — Day 1 issue

## Subject line
3 weeks, one docx, 37 chapters: what i learned writing for operators

## Preview text
the lesson wasn't about AI. it was about the order of operations.

---

three weeks ago i opened a docx to organize my AI stack notes. it became a 37-chapter site. the writing taught me more than the operating — and the lesson wasn't about AI, it was about the order of operations.

here's the operator beat from the week, separate from the book.

every time i tried to write a chapter on a tool, the chapter wouldn't close until i'd answered three questions in this exact order: what does it cost me when it works, what does it cost me when it breaks, what does it replace. tool-first writing always stalled. cost-first writing closed in a sitting.

that's the same trap operators run on themselves. you pick the tool, then you backfit a use case, then you discover the bill, then you discover the failure mode, then you cancel. four steps in the wrong order — and the third and fourth steps are the ones that should be the first and second. the chapters that worked were the ones i wrote backwards from the bill. the chapters that didn't were the ones i wrote forward from the demo.

i'm putting this in the front of every quarterly review now. before "what should we add," the question is "what does this cost when it breaks, and what does it replace." it's a 90-second filter that kills half the asks before they hit a roadmap.

— the thing i've been writing is live. field manual version of these issues — 37 chapters, no email gate. open the one that solves a problem you had this week. it's organized by problem, not curriculum, so the index is the use case.

four entry points depending on where you sit:

- /build-vs-buy — for CEOs and CTOs sequencing the next AI bet. cost ladder + 5-question matrix.
- /tier-list — for the tool-stack folks. my operator ranking next to the LMArena leaderboard.
- /research-notes — for the curious. Anthropic's 81k-interview study, DELEGATE-52, the citations behind the claims.
- /day-zero — for new operators. the folder structure, the CLAUDE.md, the first session.
